Stock Investment Playbook

A library of filing-grounded, Graham & Dodd-checked stock playbooks — one per ticker, rolled up into a P/E cheat sheet and a ranked scorecard per industry — that any investor can browse for free, and that any investor can ask a question of, in their own words, about their own portfolio as a paid upgrade.

Under the hood is an Analyst Copilot: a RAG tool trained on 600+ filing-grounded stock analyses that curates an investor's question into a rich, calibrated brief — industry-relative valuation context, comparable-company data, the Graham & Dodd margin-of-safety checklist — then hands that brief to Claude Code to produce the actual analysis. The curation is the product; Claude Code is the engine it drives.

The end consumer is an individual investor — self-directed, watchlist-keeping, ideally value-oriented — who wants filing-grounded analysis instead of stock-tip noise, without doing the SEC-filing digging themselves. 1. Executive Summary

Individual investors researching a stock today have three bad options: read the 10-K/10-Q themselves (most won't), pay for a generic research service that isn't filing-current or isn't calibrated to how a company's own industry actually trades, or ask an LLM directly — which will happily answer, but with no grounding in the filing, no sense of what "cheap" or "expensive" means for that specific industry, and no consistent analytical framework from one question to the next.

Stock Investment Playbook is a two-layer answer to that:

  • A public library of per-ticker playbooks — 10-K/10-Q-grounded income statement, segment, balance-sheet, KPI, valuation-scenario, and bull/bear sections, plus a new Graham & Dodd margin-of-safety read — rolled up into a P/E cheat sheet and a ranked scorecard for every covered industry, refreshed every quarter as new filings land.
  • An Analyst Copilot (Pro): the investor types a question in plain English — about a ticker, a comparison, or their own portfolio — and a RAG curation engine assembles the relevant playbooks, the industry's P/E and ranking context, and the Graham & Dodd checklist into one dense, structured brief, which is then handed to Claude Code to produce the actual analysis.
Revenue mechanism: a Free tier (the public playbook library) feeding a single Pro tier at $195/year with a 30-day free trial (the Analyst Copilot). No advisory fees, no assets under management, no B2B licensing in Year 1. See §10 and §11.

The product's real defensibility is not the LLM call — anyone can ask Claude a question. It's that the RAG engine has effectively been "trained" on 600+ already-built, filing-grounded, industry-calibrated analyses before it ever answers the investor's question, so the brief Claude Code receives is dramatically richer and more consistent than what an individual could assemble by prompting cold. That thesis, and a genuine compliance question about offering personalized analysis for a fee, are both tested before the Pro tier is priced and launched (§12, §14).

2. The Problem — A Prompt Is Only as Good as Its Context

Every general-purpose LLM can discuss a stock. None of them show up already knowing what "cheap" means for a warehouse club versus a semiconductor equipment maker, which one-time items in the last 10-Q should be stripped out of EPS, or how this ticker's margin of safety compares to five direct competitors. The investor has to supply all of that themselves, every time, correctly — or get a plausible-sounding but uncalibrated answer.

Research optionWhat it gives youWhat it's missing
Ask ChatGPT / Claude directlyA fluent, confident answer immediatelyNo guarantee the numbers are from the latest filing; no industry-relative calibration; no consistent framework across two tickers you ask about a week apart
Seeking Alpha / Motley Fool / Simply Wall StHuman or semi-automated commentary, sentiment, ratiosNot always filing-current; inconsistent rigor across authors; ad-driven; not personalized to your own holdings
Bloomberg Terminal / FactSetProfessional-grade, real-time data$20,000+/year — built for institutions, not a self-directed individual investor
Value LineThe closest philosophical cousin — disciplined, standardized one-page stock reportsNot interactive, not personalized, no natural-language Q&A, a legacy print-era format
Reading the 10-K/10-Q yourselfThe ground truth, fully currentHours per ticker; most investors with a watchlist of 20+ names simply don't do it every quarter
What investors are actually missing
  • Filing-grounded numbers, refreshed on the filing calendar — not a model's training-data snapshot from months or years ago.
  • Industry-relative calibration — is a 22× P/E cheap or expensive? Depends entirely on what its five closest peers trade at, and why.
  • A consistent, disciplined framework — the same margin-of-safety and earnings-quality tests applied to every ticker, not whatever the LLM feels like emphasizing that day.
  • A way to ask about their own situation — "should I rotate out of X into Y given I'm already overweight this sector" is a portfolio question a static research report can't answer.

3. A Worked Example — One Question, Two Outcomes

The question an investor types in
"I own Costco and it's run up a lot this year. I'm thinking about trimming and rotating some into Walmart, or just holding. I'm already about 60% tech in my overall portfolio and want to stay diversified. What does the data actually say?"
Asking Claude Code directly
  • Has to be told, or guess, what today's price and multiple actually are — no live filing or price feed by default.
  • Has no fixed view of what counts as "cheap" for a warehouse club versus a mass merchant — it will describe both qualitatively but without a calibrated industry P/E ladder.
  • Doesn't know the investor's actual portfolio beyond what's typed in this one message — no memory of past questions or holdings.
  • Answer quality depends heavily on how well the investor happens to phrase the prompt and which numbers they thought to paste in.
Asking through the Analyst Copilot (Pro)
  • The curation engine recognizes both tickers, pulls the current COST-PLAYBOOK and WMT-PLAYBOOK — already filing-grounded to the latest 10-Q — and the Retail P/E cheat sheet's framing of why COST trades at ~46× versus WMT's ~39×.
  • Pulls both tickers' Graham & Dodd margin-of-safety grades and the industry ranking's upside/downside context for each.
  • Folds in the investor's stated 60% tech concentration as an explicit constraint on the brief sent to Claude Code.
  • Returns an answer that cites specific filing numbers, explains the industry-relative valuation gap, and addresses the diversification question directly — in the same rigor and structure as the public playbooks, personalized to this investor's actual holdings.

The point isn't that Claude Code is smarter in the second case — it's the same model. The difference is entirely in what it was handed to work with. That handoff is the product.

5. How a Pro Analysis Request Works

End to end
1

Investor asks — a ticker, a comparison, or a portfolio question, typed in plain English; optionally attaches saved portfolio context (holdings, concentration, risk tolerance, time horizon).

2

Curation engine retrieves grounding — the matching playbook(s) or nearest comparable if the ticker isn't yet covered, the ticker's industry P/E cheat sheet and ranking, the Graham & Dodd checklist, and the investor's saved context.

3

Prompt assembly — the retrieved material is compiled into one dense, structured brief, following the same 11-section playbook framework plus the Graham & Dodd tests, with the investor's actual question framed as the task.

4

Sent to Claude Code — which pulls the live 10-K/10-Q filings and produces the analysis using the curated brief as its assignment, exactly as an analyst-authored playbook is built today.

5

Delivered & saved — a personalized HTML analysis, added to "My Analyses"; if the ticker was previously uncovered, it's queued for review to potentially join the public Free-tier library.

A filing-grounded, industry-calibrated, Graham & Dodd-checked answer to the investor's own question

The flywheel: every Pro request for a ticker not yet in the public library is a candidate to expand Free-tier coverage once reviewed — paid usage on the long tail of the 669-ticker watchlist directly subsidizes the operator's own coverage-building workload described in STOCK-INV-PIPELINE-PLAN.md's rollout phases.

12. Go-to-Market — Validation-First

The questions Phase 0 has to answer

Two things must be validated before Pro is built past a prototype: (1) will self-directed investors pay $195/year for curated, personalized analysis rather than asking a generic LLM or reading the free library alone, and (2) can the personalized-analysis offer be structured to avoid crossing into regulated investment-adviser territory (§14) — the second question gates the first.

Phase 0 — Validate (4-6 wks)
  • Ship the Free tier (Retail library, finished per §8 Phase 1); get organic traffic and community feedback
  • Get securities counsel's read on the personalized Pro concept before writing a line of billing code
  • Concierge the Pro experience by hand (curated prompt assembled manually, run through Claude Code by the operator) for 10-15 real investors
  • Kill/rework criterion: if counsel says the concept needs registration to charge for it as scoped, or if concierge users say "I'd have just asked an LLM myself," rework before building
Phase 1 — Build (only if Phase 0 validates)
  • Build the RAG curation engine, prompt-assembly pipeline, and Claude Code orchestration end to end
  • Build billing, trial logic, usage metering, and the disclaimers/ToS from §14
  • Grow coverage into Phase 2 industry groups
Phase 2 — Launch & Monetize
  • Turn on the 30-day trial + billing; launch via value-investing communities and newsletters
  • Content engine: public playbooks themselves double as SEO/shareable content ("why does X trade at 2x Y's multiple")
  • Convert Free-tier readers into trials
Phase 3 — Optimize
  • Improve trial → paid conversion with real funnel data
  • Tune the included analysis allowance against actual usage/cost data
  • Re-run the financial model (§16) with actuals

Core channel: organic, content-led distribution — the public playbook library itself is the marketing, since a well-ranked "why does COST trade at 46× while WMT trades at 39×" page draws exactly the audience Pro is built for. Paid acquisition switches on only after Phase 2 shows a conversion rate worth spending against.

14. Regulatory Positioning — Research Tool, Not Investment Advice

This is the risk that most distinguishes this business from a typical SaaS or content product, and it must be resolved before Pro is priced, not after launch. In the U.S., charging a fee for analysis of specific securities can implicate the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 (and state equivalents) if the output amounts to individualized investment advice. Two facts push in opposite directions here:

  • Working in this business's favor: the "publisher's exclusion" generally protects bona fide, regularly disseminated publications of general and impersonal advice — which describes the Free-tier public playbook library well.
  • Working against a naive version of Pro: the exclusion does not reliably cover advice tailored to an individual's specific portfolio, risk tolerance, and holdings — which is exactly what the worked example in §3 describes the Pro tier doing.

This is a genuine legal question, not a copy-editing fix, and this plan does not resolve it — it flags it as the first Phase 0 deliverable.

Design choices that keep Pro closer to "research," further from "advice"
  • Frame every output as an analysis brief in the public playbook's own format — sections, numbers, scenarios, bull/bear — applied to a ticker or scenario the user specifies, never phrased as an explicit "you should buy/sell/hold" recommendation.
  • Treat portfolio inputs as context for calibration, not as the basis for a personalized recommendation — e.g. "here is how concentrated a further COST purchase would make a tech-heavy portfolio, per the numbers" rather than "you should trim COST."
  • Minimize retention of sensitive portfolio detail — store only what's needed to calibrate an answer, not a full brokerage-linked holdings record, unless and until compliance review supports doing more.
  • Explicit, prominent disclaimers on every page and every Pro output: research and education only, not a fiduciary relationship, not personalized investment advice, the user makes their own decisions.
  • Consult securities counsel on the final product shape before launch — potentially registering as an investment adviser (with the compliance burden that implies) is itself a legitimate path if Phase 0 shows strong demand for more explicitly personalized advice than the "research brief" framing above allows.

Bottom line for this plan: Phase 0 (§12) is scoped to include a legal review as a first-class deliverable, not an afterthought. The financial model in §16 and the milestones in §18 both assume this review happens before Pro billing goes live.

19. Deep Dive — The RAG Curation & Prompt-Assembly Engine

The engine's job is narrow and specific: turn a loosely-phrased investor question into the same quality of brief a disciplined human analyst would hand a junior colleague — grounded, comparable, and framed against a fixed checklist. It is retrieval and assembly, not a separate model that "knows finance" on its own; the actual analysis is still produced by Claude Code against real filings.

Pipeline
  1. Classify the question along the axes in §7 — ticker(s), industry group, question type, time horizon, risk tolerance, whether portfolio context was supplied.
  2. Retrieve — pull the matching ticker's metrics-JSON and full playbook (or the nearest comparable tickers if uncovered), the industry's P/E cheat sheet, the industry ranking, and the Graham & Dodd checklist.
  3. Consistency check — flag staleness (e.g. the cached playbook is older than the ticker's last filed 10-Q) and route to on-demand rebuild instead of serving stale numbers.
  4. Assemble — compose a single brief: the investor's actual question, the retrieved grounding material, the fixed 11-section playbook structure, and the 8-point Graham & Dodd checklist as explicit instructions.
  5. Hand off to Claude Code — which fetches the live filings referenced, performs the actual analysis, and writes the response in the same structured format as the public playbooks.
  6. Emit — the HTML/PDF analysis, a machine-readable summary appended to the investor's saved history, and (if the ticker was uncovered) a flag for the coverage-review queue.
Example rule fragments
question_type == compare AND ticker_A.industry_group == ticker_B.industry_group → retrieve: both playbooks + shared industry PE cheat sheet + shared ranking page ticker not in coverage_index → route: on-demand playbook build (pull comparable tickers in same industry_group for interim context) portfolio_context.sector_concentration[industry_group] > 0.4 → inject: explicit concentration-risk framing into the brief, cite the investor's own stated exposure playbook.last_refreshed < ticker.last_filed_10Q → flag: STALE — trigger refresh before answering, do not serve cached numbers silently
How the engine improves
  • User feedback on each answer — a simple "was this grounded in what you needed" rating surfaces missing retrieval rules fastest.
  • Coverage-review queue — every on-demand playbook build is a candidate for the public library, and a review pass catches quality drift before it publishes.
  • Cost-per-analysis tracking — ties directly into the unit-economics risk in §16-17; retrieval rules that reduce unnecessary context (and therefore token spend) without hurting answer quality are an ongoing priority, not a one-time optimization.
  • Quarterly refresh discipline — the same "update mode" refresh described in the pipeline plan keeps the retrieval corpus current, which is what keeps the engine's answers current.

20. Summary

Stock Investment Playbook is a public library of filing-grounded, industry-calibrated, Graham & Dodd-checked stock playbooks — built on the exact pipeline already proven on 6 Retail tickers and designed to scale across the full 669-ticker watchlist on a quarterly refresh cycle. The Free tier is that library, and it is the acquisition engine. The Pro tier, at $195/year with a 30-day trial, is an Analyst Copilot: a RAG curation engine that turns an investor's own question into a rich, calibrated brief for Claude Code, drawing on everything the knowledge bank has already learned from analyzing 600+ stocks — a materially better starting point than an individual prompting an LLM cold.

The plan is validation-gated on two fronts: a commercial test (will investors pay for curated context over a free library or a raw LLM prompt) and a regulatory test (can personalized analysis be sold this way without triggering investment-adviser registration) — both resolved in Phase 0 before the Pro tier is built past a prototype. Financials in §16 are illustrative until that evidence exists, and explicitly account for a real, per-use compute cost that most SaaS financial models don't have to.
