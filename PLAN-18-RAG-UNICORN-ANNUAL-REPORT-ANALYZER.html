1. Executive Summary

The problem. An annual report runs from 100 to over 500 pages, and the information that matters most to an analyst sits in the notes: tax positions, contingent liabilities, related parties, leases, pensions, segment data. Every country, regulator and decade lays these documents out differently. A general AI chat tool can read a PDF, but it cannot tell the analyst which pages it used, whether the balance sheet it read actually balances, or whether a figure was invented. Professionals cannot put an unverifiable number in a credit memo.

The product. Annual Report Analyzer is a harness around Claude Code. An Ingester reads each report once and writes a Report Package: its statements, notes, figures, the links between statement lines and notes, and automatic checks. An Analyzer answers questions from that package and never reads the PDF. Claude picks the notes, the analyst confirms them, and the answer arrives with its source pages, linked figures and an Impact on the financial statements section.

What already exists. This is not an idea on paper. The prototype at ANN-RPT-ANALYZER\WEB-APP reads 8 report formats across the USA, India, Canada and Australia, from a 1968 microfilm scan of a Berkshire Hathaway 10-K to 2025–26 filings. It has about 17,700 lines of Python and 455 passing tests. A report it cannot read safely is refused with a gap report rather than guessed at.

The scale question. The prototype proves the design. A universal tool needs hundreds of formats. This plan's central bet is a Format Flywheel: every refused report becomes a candidate model document, and Claude Code writes most of the new extraction rules under regression tests. If adding a format costs hours rather than weeks, coverage compounds into the moat.

Business model in one line: individual analysts pay $49 a month and bring their own Claude; research teams pay about $1,700 per seat; banks, credit funds and audit firms pay about $200,000 a year for a private, audited deployment with pre-ingested coverage; and verified report data is licensed through an API.
Honest verdict: the working prototype makes this the most evidence-backed of Plans 15–18, but it sits in a crowded, well-funded category (AlphaSense, Rogo, Hebbia, Daloopa) and faces a real platform risk: Anthropic itself launched finance agents in May 2026.6 This plan estimates about a 3% chance of reaching $1B. The downside is limited: Stage 0 costs about $25,000 and six months, and a profitable niche tool for footnote-heavy analysts is a realistic fallback.

2. The Problem — Long, Varied and Hard to Verify

Analysts have always said the story is in the footnotes. The problem is getting to them. The three sample reports measured for this plan hold 1.2 to 1.4 million characters of text each (Bharat Forge FY2026: 508 pages; Chubb 10-K 2025: 460 pages; BHP 20-F 2025: 363 pages). A careful read of the notes alone takes a day. AI should make that faster, but three gaps stop professionals from trusting it.

Gap 1: Every report is different

A modern Indian report numbers its notes 1–50 with a Notes column on each statement. A US 10-K has no Notes column. A 1977 Indian report uses lettered Schedules. A 1994 EDGAR filing builds tables from spaces and dot leaders. A 1968 report is a photograph.

A tool tuned to one layout silently misreads the others.

Gap 2: Answers cannot be checked

A chat answer rarely says which pages it used, whether it read the consolidated or standalone accounts, or whether the figures it quoted tie to the statements.

A 2026 benchmark that removed figures from a profit and loss statement found models varied widely in whether they admitted the gap or invented a number.8

Gap 3: Coverage stops at large, recent, US companies

Structured data vendors concentrate on the companies their clients trade most. Daloopa, for example, reports more than 5,500 companies.4

Small caps, emerging markets and any report from before machine-readable filing are mostly left to manual reading.

The real need: analysts do not need another chatbot. They need an answer they can defend: the right notes, from the right set of accounts, with figures tied back to the statements and every page cited. That is a harness problem, not a model problem.

Design decisions that already point to scale
Ingester / Analyzer split

The Analyzer never imports the PDF library; a test enforces it. Packages can be built once and shared.

Report Package

One SQLite file per report, keyed by the PDF's SHA-256, with text, figures, links and checks. Exportable between machines.

Refuse rather than guess

Each report is fingerprinted and must pass a model's checks (notes numbered from 1, a balancing balance sheet…). If none pass, it is refused with a gap report.

Prompt-identity tests

Every prompt for every sample report is frozen. A rule added for one format is proven not to change another.

Feedback with an audit trail

Analysts flag a wrong page extraction; the fix is recorded with rule versions, tests and a timestamp.

Notes and narrative

Two flows: footnote analysis with linked figures, and business analysis from MD&A, board's report and risk factors.

Why this matters for investors: the hardest technical claim, that one design can read reports across countries and six decades without silent errors, has a working demonstration. The open question is no longer "can it work" but "can it scale cheaply," which Stage 0 is designed to answer.

4. A Worked Example — One Question, Two Ways

The question that started the prototype: "Analyze the company's tax-related liabilities and comment on it," asked of the Bharat Forge FY2026 integrated report.

Upload the PDF to a chat tool
  • The model receives roughly 1.27 million characters, about 300,000 tokens, or a truncated part of them.
  • It may read the standalone tax note, the consolidated one, or a mix, and may not say which.
  • Figures are quoted without being tied to the balance sheet or P&L.
  • Page references, if any, cannot be confirmed without reopening the PDF.
  • Every follow-up resends or re-searches the whole document.
Ask Annual Report Analyzer
  • Defaults to the consolidated accounts unless the question says otherwise.
  • Claude picks Note 21 from the note titles; the statements suggest it too, because the lines Current tax and Deferred tax liabilities link to it.
  • The analyst confirms Note 21 (PDF pages 405–407) before anything is analysed.
  • Claude receives the note, the four consolidated statements and the LINKED FIGURES, and is told to verify them.
  • The answer ends with Impact on the financial statements, and the exact source extract is one click away.

What the harness sends to Claude, in outline (from the prototype's prompt structure):

NOTES            Note 21 — Income taxes            PDF 405–407 (printed 402–404)
STATEMENTS       Profit and Loss · Balance Sheet · Cash Flow · Changes in Equity (consolidated)
LINKED FIGURES   Current tax            → Note 21   found by: Notes column
                 Deferred tax liabilities → Note 21   found by: Notes column + same figure in note
CHECKS           Subtotals add up ✓ · Balance sheet balances ✓ · Carried figures agree ✓
INSTRUCTION      Verify every linked figure against the note text; say so if one does not tie.

5. The Vision — Any Report, Any Country, Any Era

Any analyst, anywhere, can drop in any annual report and within a minute be asking expert questions of it, with every answer traceable to a page and every figure checked against the statements.

Five years from now, the product should cover the annual reports of listed companies in the 30 largest markets, in their original languages, back to the earliest digitised filings. It should also become the standard way AI agents read company reports, through an open, versioned Report Package format.

Universal input

Hundreds of model formats: IFRS, US GAAP, Ind AS, J-GAAP, HGB and older local rules; typeset, typewriter and scanned; English first, then Japanese, German, French, Spanish, Portuguese and Chinese.

Trustworthy output

Every answer cites pages, ties figures to statement lines, runs automatic checks and, where filings carry XBRL tags, cross-checks against them. Where it cannot be sure, it says so.

Wherever analysts work

Desktop app, a Claude Code and Cowork plugin, an MCP server for any agent, Excel export, a team web app, and an API for verified report data.

6. The Product & How It Works

The analyst's flow
1. Drop in a report

Any PDF: typeset, typewriter or scanned.

2. Fingerprint and match

Compared with every model document; the three closest get a trial extraction and must pass their checks.

Match

Ingest once into a Report Package (about 16 s for 500 pages; scans transcribed by Claude first).

No match

Refused, with a gap report. Feeds the Format Flywheel (section 7).

3. Ask a question

Identify notes for footnote analysis, or Analyze non notes for MD&A and board's-report passages.

4. Confirm the sources

Claude proposes notes and passages; the statements suggest more; the analyst adds or removes.

5. Verified analysis

Claude Code answers from the selected sources plus statements, linked figures and checks.

6. Follow up, save, share

Threads, history, styled HTML and Markdown reports, and the exact source extract.

Screens already built
  • Analyze: the six-step flow above, with history.
  • Ingest: package status, re-ingest, export and import, page images.
  • View statements: every statement rebuilt as a table.
  • Formatted document: the whole report as extracted, with links from statement lines to notes.
  • Model testing: test any page, flag an error, track the fix.
  • Review transcription: scan image beside its transcription.
  • Gap report: what a new format needs.
Why "harness": the tool does the deterministic work, finding, extracting, linking and checking, and Claude does the reasoning. Claude runs with no tools and only sees what it is sent. As models improve, the answers improve; the harness keeps them honest.

8. Why Now

Models can now reason about accounts

In May 2026 Anthropic launched ten finance agent templates, including earnings analysis and financial-statement review, shipped as plugins for Claude Cowork and Claude Code.6 The reasoning is good enough; what is missing is reliable input.

Claude Code is a harness platform

Headless runs, plugins, skills and MCP let a specialised tool sit on top of the analyst's own Claude. That keeps model cost off this company's books for individual users.

Buyers are spending

AlphaSense raised $350M at a $7.5B valuation in June 2026.1 Rogo raised a $160M Series D led by Kleiner Perkins in April 2026, reportedly at about $2B,2 and Daloopa raised a $47M Series C in May 2026 to supply source-linked filing data for AI agents.4

Trust is the bottleneck

Finance-specific hallucination benchmarks published in 2026 show models still differ in whether they invent missing figures.8 A verification layer is now the differentiator, not raw model access.

Move settings-only profiles out of Python into data files.

MCP server

Expose the Report Package (notes, statements, linked figures, checks) to Claude Code and other agents.

One-click desktop install building on the existing exe.

Start with SEC filings; the prototype's XBRL folder is the seed.

17. Strategic Advantage / Moat

1. The Format Library

Each model document comes with rules, acceptance checks and golden tests. Every refused report from any user makes the library bigger, and a late entrant must rebuild all of it.

2. Verified package corpus

Checked, page-linked packages for thousands of companies and decades, including scanned reports transcribed once and reused. Deep history is hard to copy quickly.

3. Regression discipline

Frozen prompts for every model prove that adding format 400 did not break format 12. That is what lets coverage grow without accuracy falling.

4. Audit trail of trust

Every flagged error and its fix is recorded. Enterprises buy the record as much as the software.

5. Model-neutral by design

Better models improve the answers without replacing the harness, as long as Kill Test 1 keeps passing on each new release.

6. Open package standard

If agents everywhere read reports through the package format, the company that writes the most packages benefits most.

Honest limit: none of this is patent-protected, and a well-funded rival could copy the design. The moat is speed and breadth: reaching hundreds of formats and a trusted record before rivals decide the long tail is worth it.

20. Summary

  • Problem: annual reports are long, their formats vary by country and decade, and AI answers about them cannot be checked.
  • Product: a Claude Code harness that ingests any matching report into a verified Report Package and answers questions with cited pages, linked figures and automatic checks.
  • Proof: a working prototype reads 8 formats across 4 countries, from 1968 to 2026, with 455 passing tests, and refuses what it cannot read safely.
  • Scale bet: the Format Flywheel turns refused reports into new formats at low cost, growing from 8 to 600 formats.
  • Business: $49 analyst plan, team seats, ~$200k enterprise deployments and a data API; modeled $134.5M run-rate in Year 5.
  • Next step: six months of Stage 0, with five kill tests. Kill Test 1, whether the harness clearly beats raw Claude, decides everything.
Bottom line: this is the rare unicorn plan whose hardest technical claim is already demonstrated. The venture is worth pursuing if, and only if, the harness proves measurably more trustworthy than the models alone and new formats prove cheap to add.
