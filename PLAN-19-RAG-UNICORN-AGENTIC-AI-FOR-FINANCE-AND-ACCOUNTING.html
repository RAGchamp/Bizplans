1. Executive Summary

The problem. Finance and accounting professionals are starting to use AI agents like Claude Code, and in May 2026 Anthropic shipped finance agent templates for this work.1 But a general-purpose agent decides for itself what to read, its quality varies from run to run, its figures are not tied to the source, and its cost is neither known nor controlled. That is acceptable for a first draft. It is not acceptable for an investment memo, a set of books or a tax worksheet.

The insight, from two working agents. Both were built with Claude Code on the same core, and both follow the same rule: code does everything that has one right answer; the model does only the judgment, and a person approves.

  • Analyze mode: the Annual Report Analyzer (Plan 18). On Travelers' 2025 10-K it read only five selected notes in one tool-free call, yet tied every figure to the statements, cited every page and found three filing anomalies a plain Claude Code session missed.3
  • Record mode: the Small Business Accounting Agent. It turns a business's scanned receipts, statements, checks, cash sheets and payroll reports into proposed journal entries. The owner approves them, and code posts them to a strict double-entry ledger and produces the statements and draft tax worksheets. On a simulated month of 74 documents, its evaluation scored 825 of 825 fields, with zero figures silently wrong and zero money double-counted. That run replayed Claude's answers from the answer key, so a live-Claude run is a Stage 0 kill test.6

The framework. The two apps already share code: the accounting agent was built by reusing the Analyzer's Claude client, job runner, ingestion loop and duplicate detection. They will be merged into the RAGchamp agent harness framework, one tested core with two modes. Analyze is read-only and produces cited analysis. Record writes into a system of record and adds approval gates, posting checks, reversals, period locks and an append-only audit trail. The two apps become the framework's reference agents.

The product. Agentic AI for Finance & Accounting Professionals teaches learners to build such agents themselves. A shared four-week core teaches the harness, then learners choose the Analyst track or the Accountant track for four more weeks, building on the matching reference agent. Every agent is graded on an Agent Scorecard of quality and cost. The Accountant track is designed to earn CPE credit, which CPAs need every year.

Business model in one line: professionals pay $495 a year for self-paced labs and starter kits (with CPE for CPAs), or $1,495 for a live cohort; a $395 certification proves the skill; research teams and accounting firms pay about $1,800 per seat; and banks, asset managers and large firms pay about $150,000 a year for a private academy.
Honest verdict: training companies rarely become unicorns. Two audiences double the market but also split a solo founder's focus, and Record-mode agents carry real liability if they post wrong entries. The plan works only if harnessed agents clearly beat plain agents in both modes, if professionals can build them, and if employers buy at scale. It estimates about a 2% chance of reaching $1B. The downside is small: Stage 0 costs about $20,000 and six months, and live cohorts in two tracks are a realistic $240,000-a-year solo fallback.

2. The Problem — Professionals Use Agents, but Cannot Build Them

Analysts turn long filings into a defensible view, again every quarter for every name they cover; the Travelers FY2025 10-K used in this plan's test is 221 pages.3 Accountants turn a shoebox of receipts, statements and reports into books that balance and taxes that can be filed, again every month for every client. Agents can now do much of this work. Three gaps stop professionals from relying on them.

Gap 1: Quality varies

A general agent chooses for itself what to read and how to act. Two runs of the same task can differ, and figures are rarely tied back to the source or cited.

For an analyst, an untraceable memo. For an accountant, an entry that silently books the same money twice.

Gap 2: Cost is unknown

An agent that re-reads everything for each task spends many times the tokens it needs. Across a coverage list or a client base, every month or quarter, that adds up, and nobody is measuring it.

In the Travelers test, the plain session's cost was not recorded at all.

Gap 3: Nobody teaches building

AI training for finance and accounting teaches prompting and vendor features. Few courses teach professionals to design an agent: what code should do, what the model should do, what a person must approve, and how to test and price each run.

So professionals depend on vendors' closed agents, or on engineers who do not know the accounting.

The real need: analysts know which footnotes matter; accountants know which account a receipt belongs in and what makes a reconciliation fail. Engineers know how to build harnesses. The missing piece is a framework that lets finance and accounting professionals encode their expertise into agents, with Claude Code writing the code, a person approving what matters, and quality and cost measured on every run.

4. The RAGchamp Agent Harness Framework — Analyze and Record

Deterministic first, AI in the middle, a person at the gate. Code does everything that has one right answer: finding, extracting, linking, checking, posting and computing. The model does only the judgment, and sees only what that judgment needs. A person approves before anything is relied on.

The framework is built by merging the shared core of the two reference agents. The Analyzer already handles 8 report formats, 4 countries and filings from 1968 to 2026;4 the Accounting Agent already handles 9 document types, scans from clean to adversarial, and a full accounting cycle through to draft tax worksheets.6

Two modes, one core
Analyze mode read-only

The agent reads sources and produces analysis. Nothing it does changes a system of record. A bad answer costs a rerun.

  • Output: a cited memo, playbook page or working paper
  • Gate: the analyst confirms sources before the analysis call
  • Key measures: figure accuracy, citations, red flags found, cost per analysis
  • Reference agent: Annual Report Analyzer
  • Typical users: equity and credit analysts, FP&A, auditors reviewing filings
Record mode writes to the books

The agent proposes changes to a system of record (a ledger, a tax worksheet). A bad answer that slips through misstates the books, so the harness adds controls.

  • Output: proposed journal entries, reconciliations, draft tax figures
  • Gate: nothing posts without approval; code refuses unbalanced or unmatched proposals
  • Controls: append-only ledger, reversals instead of edits, period locks, audit trail, dedupe and match hints
  • Key measures: silently wrong figures (must be 0), double-counted money (must be 0), entries accepted without edits, cost per document
  • Reference agent: Small Business Accounting Agent

The seven stages of a harnessed agent
1. Ingest once

Code reads the source into a package: a filing's notes and figures, or a receipt's page images and CSV lines. No model where code can do it.

2. Check

Code verifies: subtotals add up, statements balance, a receipt's items, tax and tip equal its total, a statement's balances roll forward.

3. Select or match

Analyze: a small model call picks sources from titles. Record: code finds open items and hands the model match hints.

4. A person confirms

The analyst confirms sources, or the owner reviews the extraction. Expertise enters before money or trust is spent.

5. Assemble minimal context

Only what the task needs, plus check results and instructions: verify, cite, follow the schema.

6. Model call

Analyze: one tool-free analysis call. Record: a proposal that code checks and a person approves before it posts.

7. Save, test, measure

Prompt and answer saved; prompts frozen under tests; quality and cost recorded on every run.

8. CPE Credit — The Accountant Track's Distribution Lever

Licensed CPAs must keep up continuing professional education. AICPA members, for example, must complete 120 CPE hours in each three-year period, and state boards set their own annual and cycle rules.8 Courses from sponsors on NASBA's National Registry of CPE Sponsors are accepted, to some extent, by every jurisdiction, with self-paced courses registered as QAS Self Study.9

Why it matters

CPAs and their firms already budget time and money for CPE every year. A course that earns CPE is bought from an existing budget, not a new one, and fills a requirement as well as teaching a skill.

What is planned

Register as a Registry sponsor; offer the self-paced Accountant track as QAS Self Study and live cohorts as group-internet CPE, about 30 CPE hours for the full eight weeks; issue certificates through the platform.

What it costs

Sponsor application and annual fees, course documentation to NASBA standards (objectives, review questions, final exams, attendance records), and a reviewer with CPA credentials. State rules vary and some boards add conditions.

The edge over Plan 18-style tools: analysts have no requirement as strong as CPE. The Accountant track can be sold through state CPA societies and CPE marketplaces from day one, which is why it may become the lead track once its live evaluation passes. CPE hours are estimates until NASBA standards are applied to the final course.

9. Why Now

Agents can now do finance work

In May 2026 Anthropic launched finance agent templates, including earnings analysis and financial-statement review, as plugins for Claude Cowork and Claude Code.1 The reasoning is good enough; input control, checks, approval and cost control are what professionals must add.

Non-programmers can now build

Claude Code writes, tests and explains code from a brief. Both reference agents were built this way. A professional who can specify what an agent should do, and check the result, can now build one.

Accounting has fewer people for the same work

NASBA counted 653,408 actively licensed CPAs in August 2025, down from 671,855 a year earlier.7 Firms need each accountant to do more, with controls they can defend. Agent-building is a direct answer.

AI spend and AI risk are under scrutiny

As agent use spreads, firms ask what each analysis or document costs and whether it can be trusted, and auditors ask how AI-booked entries are controlled. Agents that report both numbers and keep an audit trail are easier to approve.

18. Strategic Advantage / Moat

1. Two working agents, one core

A filings analyzer across 8 formats and 4 countries, and a bookkeeping agent through to draft taxes. Rivals teaching agent building have no proven finance harness, let alone one covering both modes.

2. Benchmarks with answer keys

Hand-checked filing questions, and a generator that produces bookkeeping sets from a known true ledger with every scan defect. Slow and expensive to copy well.

3. Quality-cost data

Every learner agent run shows which design choices raise quality or cut cost, in both modes. That data improves the kits and the curriculum.

4. Agents that stay in use

Accountants run their agents every month; analysts every quarter. The kits and library keep them subscribed after the course.

5. CPE and certification

CPE turns a course into a recurring requirement; certification creates a network effect once employers ask for certified agent builders.

6. Model-neutral method

Deterministic-first design holds whichever model is best. Better models improve the agents; they do not remove the need for checks, approval, audit trails and cost control.

Honest limit: the framework's ideas are not secret, and a large CPE provider, training brand or AI lab could teach something similar. The moat is the working agents, the benchmarks and the agents learners keep running, and only if they are kept current faster than rivals.

21. Summary

  • Problem: finance and accounting professionals use general agents whose quality varies and whose cost is unknown, and nobody teaches them to build better ones.
  • Evidence: two working agents on one core. The Annual Report Analyzer produced deeper, tied-out, cited analysis of Travelers' 10-K from five selected notes. The Small Business Accounting Agent scored 825 of 825 fields with zero silent errors and zero double-counting on a simulated month, in replay mode; a live run comes next.
  • Framework: the two apps merged into the RAGchamp agent harness framework, with an Analyze mode for read-only analysis and a Record mode that adds approval gates, posting checks, reversals, period locks and an audit trail.
  • Product: a four-week shared core, then an Analyst track or an Accountant track, with starter kits, benchmarks and an agent library, graded on an Agent Scorecard of quality and cost. The Accountant track is designed to earn CPE.
  • Business: $495 self-paced, $1,495 cohorts, $395 certification, $1,800 team and firm seats, ~$150k academies; modeled $150.2M revenue in Year 5; about $155M of funding; ~2% odds of $1B.
  • Next step: six months of Stage 0 with six kill tests. Kill Tests 1 and 2 decide whether each mode is worth teaching, and which track leads.
Bottom line: extending the framework to accountants makes the plan bigger and the evidence broader, and CPE gives it a ready-made channel. The risk is focus: build one core, prove both modes, then launch one track at a time.
