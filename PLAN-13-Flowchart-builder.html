Executive Summary

Consultants, engineers, trainers and product teams regularly need diagrams that are more than boxes and arrows: lifecycle loops (MLOps, DevOps, product, customer journeys), interlocking "knot" diagrams, value chains and roadmaps drawn as thick curved ribbons that weave over and under each other, with each stage in its own color and its label running along the curve. PowerPoint, Excel, Publisher and SmartArt cannot build these. Today people either fight with freeform shapes for hours, pay a designer, or settle for a plain box diagram that does not tell the story.

There is already a working MVP of the hardest part of this problem, in FLOW-CHART-APP/App/index.html: a single-file, no-install browser app (about 2,260 lines, no libraries, works offline) that draws thick Arc and Bézier ribbons, joins them into one continuous figure with Snap it (closing gaps, trimming overlaps, smoothing every join), cuts the figure into colored, labelled pieces by clicking Arrows onto it, keeps labels upright along the curve, lets the user choose which piece runs on top where the ribbon crosses itself, and saves the whole diagram as JSON. It has already recreated the reference "MLOps: Eternal Knot" diagram. What it lacks is everything that turns a tool into a business: export into decks, templates, accounts, and a faster way to start than drawing every band by hand.

Flow Chart Builder turns that editor into a product by adding export (high-resolution PNG, SVG, PDF and editable PowerPoint), a template gallery, a cloud library, and an Agentic AI layer: AI Trace converts a photo or screenshot of a flow chart into editable bands, and Describe-to-Diagram builds a ribbon layout from a plain-English list of stages. The AI only proposes shapes in the app's own JSON format; the user's editor stays fully editable, so nothing is ever locked into a flat image.

A free tier built on the already-working editor, and a $99/year Pro subscription that unlocks deck-ready export, templates, cloud sync and the AI features. Year-1 target: ~60,000 free signups → 1,800 Pro subscribers (3% conversion) ≈ $178,200 in annual recurring revenue.

Against an estimated $44,146 in total Year-1 operating costs (LLM inference for the AI features, marketing, cloud hosting, payment processing, template content, and basic legal/LLC setup), this lands at an estimated ~$134,054 pre-tax profit (≈75% margin), and roughly ~$93,838 after an assumed ~30% tax rate (see Net Profit Model).

2. The Problem

  • Office tools are built for boxes and straight connectors. PowerPoint, Excel, Publisher and SmartArt offer rectangles, elbow connectors and a few fixed cycle layouts. A thick ribbon that loops, crosses itself and changes color per stage is not one of their shapes.
  • Hand-building it with freeform shapes takes hours and breaks on every edit. People stitch arcs, block arcs and freeform curves together, nudge them pixel by pixel to hide the seams, and then start over when a stage is added or renamed.
  • Text cannot follow a curve in a usable way. Office's "follow path" WordArt is limited and fragile, so labels end up as rotated text boxes floating near the ribbon instead of on it.
  • General diagramming tools solve a different problem. Visio, Lucidchart, draw.io and Miro are excellent at process maps, org charts and BPMN, but their curves are thin connectors, not wide ribbons that can be split into labelled pieces and woven over and under each other.
  • Design tools are too open-ended, and designers are expensive. Illustrator, Figma or a freelance designer can produce the picture, but the result is a flat image the presenter cannot edit the night before the meeting.
  • Nobody owns this category. A focused, easy editor for ribbon-style flow charts that drops straight into a slide deck does not exist today. That gap is the business opportunity.

3. The Product — Ribbon Editor + AI Layer

What's already built — the ribbon editor MVP

A working, single-file browser app at FLOW-CHART-APP/App/index.html, with no server, no install and no AI involved:

ComponentWhat it does today
Arc & Bézier toolsDraw thick ring segments (three clicks: center, start, end) and S-curve bands (start, end, then bend the control points). Ends snap to other ends and centers snap to centers within 12 px, so ring segments line up and curves continue without a kink
Snap itJoins every hand-drawn band into one continuous figure: pairs ends that face each other, bridges gaps with a smooth curve, trims overlaps, and closes loops. The whole snap is one undo step
Arrow toolEach click on a figure adds a chevron and cuts the figure into a new piece. Four arrow styles (Chevron, Triangle, Notch, Thin chevron cut), plus start/end arrows

Weaving (z-order)Where a figure crosses itself, Bring piece to front / Send piece to back chooses which piece runs on top, and the choice is saved
Icons, badges, titles16 built-in icons or an uploaded picture with a label, self-numbering circle badges, free text, and a title + subtitle block
Trace imageLoad a picture of an existing flow chart as a faded background and draw over it; clicks pass through so it is never selected
Save / load / undoJSON save and load with validation, 200-step undo/redo, browser autosave with "Restored your last session", a built-in example diagram, pan and zoom from 10% to 800%

What is missing is export beyond JSON, which is why PowerPoint export fidelity is a key build item.

4. Target Market

Primary buyer/user: people who explain processes and lifecycles in slides for a living: management and technology consultants, solution architects and ML/data/DevOps engineers, corporate trainers and online course creators, product and program managers, and marketing teams producing explainer graphics. A secondary population is students and educators who need one striking diagram for a thesis, paper or lecture.

1. Consultants & strategy / PMO teams
Live in PowerPoint; a striking framework diagram is part of the deliverable. Highest willingness to pay, and $99/year is below any approval threshold.
2. Engineers, architects & tech educators
MLOps, DevOps, data and AI lifecycle diagrams are exactly the "eternal knot" shape the MVP was built for; they publish them in blogs, talks and courses.
3. Trainers, creators, PMs & students
Large, price-sensitive, high-volume: the natural Free-tier population and the source of the "Made with" viral loop, with a meaningful Pro minority.

Out of scope for Year 1: formal diagram standards (BPMN, UML, network diagrams), org charts, whiteboarding and real-time multi-user editing. That is Visio / Lucidchart / Miro territory, and competing there would dilute the product. This is a niche-but-recurring-need market: the same people build a new deck every few weeks, which supports subscription (not one-time) pricing, backed by the cloud library and monthly template drops.

13. Strategic Advantage / Moat

  • The hard geometry is already built. Snap it (pairing ends, bridging gaps with smooth curves, trimming overlaps, closing loops), cutting a figure into pieces with arrows, upright text along curves and piece-level weaving are what make ribbon diagrams easy. They are working today in the MVP, which materially de-risks and shortens the Year-1 build.
  • Category focus. General diagram and design tools are broad by design; Flow Chart Builder does one diagram family better than anyone and can own its search terms ("ribbon diagram," "lifecycle loop," "knot diagram") through the template gallery.
  • Complements PowerPoint instead of fighting it. Editable PPTX export means users keep their existing deck workflow; the product slots in rather than asking them to switch.
  • AI that stays editable. AI Trace and Describe-to-Diagram output the app's own shapes, not images, so the result is better than a one-shot AI picture and keeps the user in the editor.
  • Built-in distribution. Every free export carries the "Made with" mark into decks, blogs and courses, in front of exactly the kind of people who make diagrams.
  • Near-zero cost to serve free users. The editor runs in the browser, so the free tier can be generous without burning cash, and gross margin on Pro stays high.
  • Price removes the approval blocker. $99/year is low enough that a consultant, engineer or trainer can expense it or pay personally, with no procurement process.

16. Summary

The plan rests on a working, already-built ribbon-diagram editor that draws the complex, curved, woven flow charts PowerPoint cannot, extended with deck-ready export, a template gallery, a cloud library and an Agentic AI layer (AI Trace and Describe-to-Diagram) that removes the blank-canvas and tracing steps, funneling into a $99/year Pro subscription. Hitting the goal of 1,800 Pro subscribers (≈$178,200 revenue) requires roughly 60,000 free signups at a 3% conversion rate, sourced mostly from template SEO pages, LinkedIn and the "Made with" watermark loop rather than paid ads. Against ~$44,146 in total Year-1 operating costs, led by AI inference and customer acquisition, this plan lands at an estimated ~$134,054 pre-tax profit, and roughly ~$93,838 after an assumed ~30% tax rate, with the already-solved ribbon geometry and a focused, PowerPoint-friendly category position as the durable competitive moat.
