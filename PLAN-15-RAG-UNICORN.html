1. Executive Summary

The fundamental problem. RAG has no concept of "in force." It ranks text by similarity, so a superseded policy can outrank the memo that replaced it. It cannot prove it found everything. And once an answer is delivered, nothing records what that answer relied on. When a source changes, no one can say which answers, decisions or agent actions are now wrong.

That was tolerable when RAG powered a search box that a person double-checked. It stops being tolerable when agents act on the answer without anyone reading it.

The product. FactLedger moves the expensive AI work from query time to ingest time. A Compiler turns documents into atomic facts. A Ledger stores each fact with its source, its effective dates, its authority and its permissions. An Answer Receipt records exactly which facts every answer used. A Recall Engine follows those links in reverse: when a fact changes, it finds what was affected, the way a manufacturer traces a faulty part to the cars that contain it.

The gap. Enterprise context is crowded: Glean, Microsoft, Snowflake, Databricks and Atlan all compete for it. Tracing answers back from a changed fact is much less occupied. The idea appears in 2026 research and engineering posts, but this plan's searches found no funded product built around it. That was a web search, not proof of absence, and Stage 0 must retest it.

The entry point. Banks and insurers come first. Their documents are versioned and contradictory, wrong answers are costly, and two changes have opened a gap: US model-risk guidance issued in April 2026 places generative and agentic AI outside its scope, and optional generative-AI exclusions for standard liability policies became available in January 2026.

Business model in one line: a fixed-price $60,000 Knowledge Audit proves the problem on a customer's own documents, then converts to an annual platform subscription that starts at $150,000 and expands one knowledge domain at a time.
Honest verdict: this is a credible venture thesis with long odds. This plan estimates about a 2% chance of reaching $1B. It is also the first plan on this site that cannot stay a solo business: it needs a technical co-founder and about $173M of modeled funding. The first six months can be done solo, and if customers pay for audits but the company cannot be funded or staffed, the audit work can continue as a solo service near the site's usual $175k revenue scale (Section 11).

2. The Problem — RAG Has No Concept of "In Force"

Better embeddings, rerankers and bigger context windows all improve how text is found. None of them changes what standard RAG fundamentally is: a stateless lookup that starts from zero on every question and forgets everything afterward. That design leaves three gaps.

Gap 1: Similar is not valid

A similarity score says nothing about which document is current, which one has authority, or whether two documents disagree.

Outdated information substantially lowers RAG accuracy even when current information is available.3 Conflicting sources are now a research field of their own.4

Gap 2: Top-k cannot prove "all"

"Which contracts contain this clause?" needs a complete answer. Top-k returns a sample and cannot tell you what it missed.

An ICLR 2026 paper shows that embedding dimension limits which result sets a single-vector model can return.1 When context falls short, models tend to answer anyway instead of abstaining.2

Gap 3: Answers leave no trail

Citations show where an answer came from at that moment. Nothing tells you later that the answer has gone bad.

This is the least-served gap. A 2026 paper calls it "change provenance" and tests it as a research prototype.8

Why larger context windows do not close the gaps: a model that reads every document can sometimes spot a contradiction, and that does weaken Gap 1 for small document sets. It still leaves no durable record, so Gap 3 remains. And a window large enough for one team's files is not large enough for an enterprise's, with its permissions, on every query.

3. A Worked Example — One Policy Change

This scenario is illustrative and invented; the numbers show the mechanism and are not measurements. A mid-size bank's service agent answers customer questions about credit card hardship programs.

The change: on March 3, a two-page memo cuts the hardship fee-waiver window from 90 days to 60, effective April 1. The 40-page policy manual and a training deck, both saying 90 days, stay on SharePoint.

March 10: "How long is the waiver?"Might return 60 from the memo, which is wrong because the change is not yet in force. Might return 90, which is right by luck.Returns 90 days, and adds that it becomes 60 on April 1. Both facts exist with effective dates.

The March 10 row is the important one. No amount of "newest document wins" logic gets it right, because the newest document is not yet in force. The system has to store two separate dates: when a fact takes effect, and when the organization learned about it.

4. The Vision — Compile Once, Answer With Proof

Software went through this shift decades ago. An interpreter re-reads source code on every run; a compiler does the hard work once, checks it, and rebuilds only what changed. Today's RAG is an interpreter for company knowledge: every question re-reads raw documents and re-derives the same conclusions, including the same mistakes.

Retrieval-time RAGCompiled knowledge
Unit of knowledgeA chunk of textA versioned fact with a source
When conflicts are handledNever, or inside each promptOnce, at ingest, with a named owner
TimeA file date, at bestEffective dates plus recorded dates
"Show me all..."A top-k sampleA structured query over known facts
Unknown questionsOften answered anywayFalls back to retrieval and is labeled unverified
After the answerNothing remains but a log lineA receipt that can be traced and recalled
Three ideas this plan combines, and credits
  • Truth maintenance (1979). Jon Doyle's truth maintenance systems tracked why each belief was held, so conclusions could be withdrawn when their support disappeared. The idea was sound, but the facts had to be entered by hand. LLMs remove that bottleneck.
  • The LLM wiki (2026). Andrej Karpathy's April 2026 proposal has an LLM compile and maintain a knowledge base instead of re-reading sources.7 It validated the pattern for individuals, without enterprise permissions, audit trails or recall.
  • Plan 14's Deterministic Layer. Use AI once to build something that then runs repeatably. FactLedger applies that split to knowledge: AI judgment at ingest, deterministic lookup and verification at answer time, and an LLM only to phrase the result.
What "true" means here: FactLedger does not decide what is objectively true. It records what the organization's authoritative sources say is in force, who said so, and from when. That is a narrower claim, and one a compliance officer can actually sign off on.

5. The Product & How It Works

Enterprise sources

Policies, contracts, regulations, tickets, wikis, databases

1. Compiler (AI at ingest)

Extracts facts, matches entities, spots conflicts and superseded versions. Sends unclear cases to a human owner.

2. Ledger (the system of record)

Each fact keeps its source, effective dates, authority and permissions. Nothing is overwritten.

3. Answer Receipts (at answer time)

Agents connect via MCP or SDK. Each answer is checked against facts and logged with what it used.

4. Recall Engine (when anything changes)

Follows receipts backward from a changed fact, ranks the impact, then alerts, re-runs or escalates.

What an Answer Receipt records
{
  "receipt_id": "rcp_8f3a",
  "answered_at": "2027-04-15T14:02:11Z",
  "agent": "card-service-agent v3.2",
  "user_scope": "tier1_service",
  "facts_used": [{
    "fact": "hardship.fee_waiver_window = 60 days",
    "version": 7,
    "in_force_from": "2027-04-01",
    "source": "memo-2027-031, para 2",
    "authority": "Policy Office"
  }],
  "coverage": "fully_grounded",
  "status": "valid"
}

Illustrative schema. The receipt format would be published as an open specification (Section 12).

The four hard engineering problems

These are where the company's real intellectual property would come from, and where it could fail.

  • Meaning-level dependency. Tracking cited documents is easy. Knowing an answer relied on an interpretation of a fact is not, and this is a recognized open question.9
  • Over-recall. If every edit triggers alerts, people will ignore them. Recall needs high precision and impact ranking.
  • Lossy extraction. Exceptions, conditions and tables can get flattened into a fact that is subtly wrong. Each fact must keep a link to its original passage, and high-stakes facts need review.
  • Inherited permissions. A fact drawn from two sources must respect the stricter rule, or the Ledger becomes a data-leak path.
Product sequence: each step works without the next
ReleaseWhat shipsWhy customers can adopt it
1. AuditCompiler run on one document domain, delivered as a conflict and staleness reportRead-only. No production integration needed.
2. Receipts LiteSDK that logs document versions for answers from an existing RAG stack, with document-level RecallWraps what they have. Nothing gets replaced.
3. LedgerFact-level storage, MCP server and steward review queue for one domainStarts where wrong answers hurt most.
4. RecallFact-level impact analysis and automated re-runsBecomes the reason to expand across domains.

FactLedger does not replace retrieval. Retrieval still finds candidates for the Compiler and handles questions the Ledger does not cover. The share of answers fully grounded in facts is a headline product metric.

7. Target Market

Stress test: can deployment scale?

The model above assumes each deployment gets cheap. That estimate is unvalidated and may be high, and a bank pays far more than a tax firm. But here is what it would mean at Year 5 volume.

What this shows: at these effort levels the company is a viable services-heavy business, not a software unicorn. Even the kinder case needs about 180 delivery people out of 320 staff and leaves margin near 55%, the Year 1 level. Deployment effort must fall about 80%, from 44 person-weeks to about 8. The root cause is a tension in the thesis: the part that scales is not defensible, and the part that is defensible does not scale. Document-level Recall deploys quickly but is easy to copy (Section 13). Fact-level compilation is the claimed advantage, but it carries the customer-specific labor.
Ways to close the gap
  • Compile on demand. Start with Receipts Lite, then compile only the facts answers actually use, plus known high-stakes facts. This is probably the largest saving, but it changes the "compile everything at ingest" idea in Section 4.
  • Share public facts. Compile regulations and tax law once for all customers, so each customer adds only its own facts.
  • Move deployment to partners. Consultancies and audit firms do the work and the customer pays them. This suits banks, but rules out smaller firms.
  • Charge for deployment. A paid implementation fee covers the cost, but it counts as services revenue, not ARR.
  • Narrow the market. Sell only to buyers large enough to absorb deployment. That likely removes the mid-size segment in Section 7 ($2.5B of the $8.3B) and casts doubt on the Year 5 self-serve edition.

Kill Test 5 in Section 11 measures this directly. Three audits are a small sample, so it shows direction, not proof.

14. Strategic Advantage / Moat

A startup has no moat on day one. These advantages build up only if the company survives long enough, roughly in the order listed.

History that cannot be rebuilt

Receipts record what was relied on at the time. A competitor can copy the software, but it cannot recreate years of a customer's past. This is the strongest lock-in, as with a general ledger.

Encoded authority

Every steward decision records how the organization ranks its own sources. That map is valuable, specific to each customer, and costly to redo elsewhere.

Neutrality

Banks run several models, clouds and agent platforms. An independent record is more credible as evidence than one kept by the vendor being audited.

Standards and evidence

Owning the benchmark and receipt specification shapes how buyers evaluate the category. With time, real error and recall data could also inform underwriting.

These are this plan's judgment calls, not statistics. Published base rates for seed-funded startups becoming unicorns run from about 1% to 2.5%, and Carta reports 5.6% for the top quartile of seed valuations.27 The 2% here equals about 3.6% for a company that clears Stage 0 and raises money.

What must all be true
  1. Buyers will pay for traceability as its own budget line, and will not wait for their existing vendor.
  2. Fact extraction reaches production-grade accuracy on messy documents.
  3. Human review shrinks enough to support 75% gross margins.
  4. Deployment effort falls about 80%, to about 8 person-weeks per domain, or partners take it on.
  5. Fact-level recall is clearly better than the simple version incumbents can ship.
  6. A top-tier technical co-founder joins.
  7. Funding markets remain open through a Series C.
Compared with Plans 1–14

Those plans aim at a likely-sized personal income with full ownership. This one trades that for a small chance of a very large outcome: a 33% founder-group stake at $1B would be worth up to about $333M on paper before preferences, taxes and the split among co-founders. It also means years of near-certain pressure, outside investors and a large team. That is a personal choice as much as a business one. The sensible sequence is to spend six months and about $25,000 to find out whether the thesis deserves that choice.

17. Summary

RAG made AI useful on private documents, but it treats knowledge as text to be searched. It has no way to say what is in force, whether it found everything, or which past answers have gone bad. As agents begin to act on those answers, this becomes a structural weakness.

FactLedger's vision is to make company knowledge something that is compiled, versioned and accountable, the way double-entry bookkeeping made money accountable. The opening is the least-occupied part of a crowded market: tracing what happens after the answer, for buyers who now have to prove their AI is under control.

Next step: begin Stage 0. Hold 20 interviews, build InForce-Bench, and let the five kill tests decide. If they pass, this becomes a venture-scale company with a roughly $1B target. If they fail at the last two stages, the audit work may still support a solo business. Either way, the next six months are affordable.
